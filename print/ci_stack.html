Where Early Models Usually Understate Cost

  • Historic rehab complexity: hidden structure, water damage, envelope issues, hazmat, and code-triggered upgrades.
  • Electrical and utility work: transformer/switchgear/interconnection upgrades are often bigger than expected.
  • Rooftop mobility scope: structural reinforcement, fire/life safety, access, and ops systems are not trivial add-ons.
  • Technology integration: dispatch/data/control systems are expensive because the integration layer is real infrastructure, not just software.
  • Commissioning and launch: integrated testing, startup support, and acceptance work are usually underbudgeted.
Section 04 · Budget Logic

How This Stack Should Be Applied

Do not present a single blended “construction/infrastructure” number without explaining the activation scope. Investors will immediately ask: how much square footage, how much TI, how much rooftop work, how much charging, and what contingency is embedded?
Budget QuestionRequired Answer
How much building is being activated in Phase 2?State the square footage explicitly and whether it is shell-only, common-area, or full TI activation.
Is the rooftop package demonstration-grade or flagship-grade?State whether the vertiport scope is minimal, credible initial, or robust multi-pad with stronger passenger/ops systems.
What is included in “technology”?Break out security, structured cabling, signage, NOC, dispatch/data integration, access control, and any custom middleware.
How are unknowns handled?Show a visible contingency line instead of hiding risk in optimistic hard-cost assumptions.

Section 05 · Recommended Investor Framing

How to Present This in Meetings

  • Lead with a phased activation narrative, not a full-building fantasy on day one.
  • Use the cost stack to show that the team understands where cost risk actually lives.
  • Present the capital-light base case separately from any owned-fleet or optional seed-fleet scenario.
  • Make clear that major construction/infrastructure numbers are planning estimates that must be validated by architect, engineer, utility, code, and contractor diligence.

Suggested Summary Language

Our current planning view is that a credible Trinity Air Link initial build likely falls into a broad $67M–$122M flagship initial-build band, depending on activation square footage, rooftop scope, utility upgrades, technology integration depth, and historic-rehab unknowns. Lower initial capital cases are only credible if the project is explicitly framed as a phased activation strategy.

What This Stack Does Not Include

  • Full vehicle fleet acquisition as a baseline assumption.
  • Long-term operating losses, debt-service structure, or working-capital runway beyond startup scope.
  • Final GMP or contractor bid pricing.
  • Formal tax-credit underwriting or detailed incentive monetization.
