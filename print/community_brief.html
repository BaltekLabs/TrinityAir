Section 01 · What Is the Link?

Transforming Fort Worth's Most Iconic Building

The Trinity Air Link Transportation System is a proposal to transform the historic Texas & Pacific Warehouse in downtown Fort Worth — the landmark 8-story, 600-foot-long 1920s railroad building — into the nation's first integrated transportation hub.

The Link will combine electric air taxis (eVTOL), autonomous electric vehicles, and a 120,000 sq ft innovation center into a single, seamlessly connected facility that preserves Fort Worth's railroad heritage while pioneering its transportation future.

This is not science fiction. On March 9, 2026, the White House announced that Texas DOT and Archer Aviation were selected as one of only 8 national sites for the federal eVTOL Integration Pilot Program. Air taxi operations begin in Texas this summer. The Link is the infrastructure that puts Fort Worth on that map.

Section 03 · Answering Your Questions

Community Q&A

Q: Are air taxis safe? Will this put aircraft over our neighborhoods?
Modern electric air taxis have extensive redundant safety systems certified by the FAA. They are 100x quieter than traditional helicopters. Flight paths are designed to minimize noise over residential areas. The technology being used — Archer Midnight and Joby S4 — has undergone years of FAA certification testing. Waymo's autonomous vehicles have 88% fewer injury claims than human drivers. Safety is the first priority, not an afterthought.
Q: Will this increase traffic and congestion around the T&P area?
The hub is specifically designed to reduce local traffic by providing alternatives to driving. Most passengers will arrive via existing rail, bus, or autonomous vehicles. Smart traffic management systems actively reduce congestion around the facility. The goal is to take cars off the road, not add them.
Q: Is this affordable for regular Fort Worth residents?
Autonomous vehicle fares ($15–25/trip) are priced comparably to current Uber/Lyft. Air taxi fares will be premium initially ($75–150/trip) but will decline as fleet scales — similar to how Uber/Lyft prices dropped after launch. Subscription programs and community discounts are planned. All Fort Worth residents benefit from reduced traffic congestion on roads.
Q: How will construction affect daily life near downtown?
Construction is phased to minimize community disruption. Local traffic management plans will be implemented. Regular updates will be provided on progress. Local hiring is prioritized for construction jobs. Construction will be coordinated around major community events in the downtown area.
Q: What tax dollars are being spent on this project?
The Link is primarily a private investment project ($129M total). Government incentives include standard federal programs (Historic Tax Credits, transportation grants) that are available to any qualifying project. The project will generate an estimated $25M+ annually in new tax revenue for the City of Fort Worth by Year 5 — a net positive for public finances.
