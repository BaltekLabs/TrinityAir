The full vision

Not simply a vertiport. A new civic gateway.

The opportunity is larger than a rooftop pad or a single vehicle type. Trinity Air Link is a platform for a future in which every mode works together—and a long-vacant Fort Worth landmark becomes useful, public-facing, and alive again.

“Turn the place that once connected Texas by rail into the place that connects the next era of movement.”Trinity Air Link north star

At its fullest expression, the T&P building could become a front door to North Texas: travelers arriving by regional rail, transferring through a managed electric curb, and continuing on qualified regional air services—all through one digital journey.

Inside, flexible space could support mobility research, robotics, artificial intelligence, charging and energy systems, operator teams, education, hospitality, and public experiences. The hub would not need to own the vehicles. Its job would be to make the network feel connected.

The place

An icon can become a gateway again.

The preferred site is the warehouse at 401 W Lancaster Avenue beside downtown rail access. Its scale, history, and location make it uniquely compelling. They also make it a serious adaptive-reuse challenge.

The vision honors that tension: preserve what makes the T&P extraordinary, open it to a new generation, and stage only the uses that can survive technical, civic, and financial scrutiny.

Secure an option, lease framework, or other enforceable diligence right.
Confirm parcel, title, access, area, condition, preservation, and parking constraints.
Test structure, fire/life safety, utilities, charging, airspace, noise, emergency response, and vertiport geometry.
Keep a ground-level or alternate downtown configuration alive if rooftop operations fail.
Current status: Trinity Air Link is in Phase 0—validation and site control. The project does not currently represent that it controls the property, has operator commitments, holds FAA operating authority, or has an approved construction plan.
